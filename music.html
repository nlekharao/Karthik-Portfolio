Beyond Engineering

Music &
DJing

Music has always been something I've enjoyed, but DJing changed the way I listen to it. It made me think about rhythm, transitions and how different tracks can come together to change the energy of a room.

“At some point, listening wasn't quite enough. I wanted to understand how tracks could work together.”

01 · Why Music

Drawn to electronic music

I've always been drawn to electronic music — the rhythm, the energy and the way different layers build over time. There is a lot happening within a track, and the more I listened, the more interested I became in how those elements work together.

02 · Why DJing

From listening to putting music together

At some point, listening wasn't quite enough. I became curious about how tracks could work together — how moving from one sound or mood to another could change the way the music felt. DJing gave me a way to engage with music rather than only listen to it.

03 · Behind the Decks

Reading the room

The best part is seeing people respond to what you're playing. You might have an idea of where you want the set to go, but you're also reacting to the room. When a transition works and you can feel the energy change, there's an immediate connection that you don't get when you're listening to music on your own.

A memorable experience

Playing in New York

I had the opportunity to play a couple of sets at events in New York. I was still DJing for fun, but playing for an actual room of people made it feel completely different. What I remember most is seeing people respond to music I had chosen and mixed — that was probably the point at which DJing became more than something I just did at home.

For me, DJing is still something I do because I enjoy it — discovering music, experimenting with what works together and occasionally getting the chance to share it with a room full of people.
